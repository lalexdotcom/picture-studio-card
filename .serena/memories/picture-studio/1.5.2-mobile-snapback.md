# 1.5.2 — the mobile snap-back, investigation in flight

> Written 2026-08-22, mid-investigation, because the session was about to be
> restarted. **Nothing here is settled.** The root cause is not known, and the
> whole point of this file is to make sure the next session does not re-derive
> the ground already covered — or, worse, "tidy away" the instrumentation that
> is the only measuring device we have.

## Read this first: the working tree is dirty ON PURPOSE

`git status` shows an untracked `src/card/drag-diagnostics.ts` and modifications
to `src/card/drag-layer.ts` and `src/card/picture-studio-card.ts`. **Do not
commit them and do not revert them.** They are temporary instrumentation, every
touch point marked `// TEMP`, and they are what the user is about to run against
their phone. The suite passes with them in (839 tests), so a green run proves
nothing about whether they are still present — grep for `TEMP` instead.

`dist/` currently holds the **instrumented** build (213.8 kB, against 210.3 kB
for the real 1.5.1). The local Home Assistant serves it: `dist/` is mounted at
`/config/www/picture-studio-card/`. So the phone pointed at the local HA gets
the instrumentation with nothing further to do.

## What happened, in order

1.5.1 shipped the selection-on-release fix (see the bullets in
`mem:picture-studio/state`) and **the user pushed it** — it is a published
release, not a candidate. Then they tested on a real phone, and found a defect.

## The symptom, in the user's own terms

An item **returns to its position from the start of the drag** after a very short
time of dragging. Everything below is a discriminator they established, and each
one costs a round trip to re-establish:

- **Systematic from a fresh load**, and it repeats when the editor is closed and
  reopened.
- **A slow drag that never leaves the item behaves the same.** So it is
  time-based, not travel-based, and not a question of leaving the element.
- **It does not happen in Chrome's "Device" mode.** This is why a local
  reproduction is the wrong instrument — see below.
- **It only happens in the PANEL view.** In their tests the sections view is
  clean.
- A workaround found by accident: touching the card once on the dashboard, then
  reopening the editor, gives the right behaviour — **but not every time**.

## The three writers of a pre-drag position, which is the whole search space

The user's own redirection, and it is the right frame: rather than reading
events, ask each place that can write an item's position to say so. There are
exactly three, and the instrumentation makes each announce itself.

| Token | Who wrote | What it would prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `restore:cancel` | `endGesture` in `drag-layer.ts`, cancelled branch | A `pointercancel`: the browser took the gesture. Would be **pre-existing**, not a 1.5.1 regression — restoring on a non-commit landed in 1.5.0. |
| `restore:click` | same branch, `isDrag` verdict negative | The gesture counted neither in travel nor in time. Would point at the clock or the move counting on mobile. |
| `rebuild` then `gone` | `_syncItems` → `layer.replaceChildren()` | The card rebuilt its children mid-gesture and destroyed the element under the finger. Fits "fresh load" and the warm-up workaround: `loadCardHelpers()` is awaited inside the rebuild. |
| `apply` | `_applyPositions` | The stored coordinates — the pre-drag ones until Home Assistant's round trip lands — were written over the item. |

Supporting tokens: `PC` (pointercancel reached the controller), `sel` (a text
selection appeared — the long-press callout of a WebView), `blur`, `lpc`
(pointer capture left on its own), `mvN` (pointermove count), `@t` in ms since
pointerdown. The overlay draws them in a black strip at the top of the preview,
in editing mode only, because **the companion app gives no console** — that
constraint is what shaped the whole approach.

## Two hypotheses, neither confirmed

**The long-press callout.** There is `touch-action: none` on `.editing .item`,
but **no `user-select: none` and no `-webkit-touch-callout: none` anywhere in
the card** — measured, the grep is empty. A WebView's long press then starts a
text selection, which cancels the pointer. Fits "time-based", fits "identical
slow or fast", fits "absent in Device mode" (which does not emulate the native
callout). Would show as `sel` then `PC` then `restore:cancel`.

**The mid-gesture rebuild.** `_syncItems` awaits `window.loadCardHelpers()` and
then calls `layer.replaceChildren()` with **no drag guard at all**. Every editor
opening builds a fresh card whose `_renderedTypes` is empty, so the rebuild path
always runs. Fits "fresh load", fits the warm-up workaround, fits "not in Device
mode" (everything is already loaded on the desktop). Would show as `rebuild`
then `gone`.

The panel-only clue is not yet explained by either, and that is the one thing
that should make anyone suspicious of both.

## What was ruled out, and what it cost

**A local reproduction cannot be the instrument, by the user's own evidence.** A
Chromium bench with touch emulation *is* Device mode, where the defect does not
occur. This was pursued far enough to open the real card editor in the panel
view of `dashboard-test` on a 390 px viewport, with real touch through CDP
`Input.dispatchTouchEvent`, before the point was conceded. The scripts survive
under `captures/` (git-ignored) — `repro.mjs`, `probe.mjs`, `probe2.mjs` — and
they are worth keeping only for their plumbing, not their verdict.

Two facts that bench did establish and that are cheap to lose:

- **The dashboard's `url_path` is `dashboard-test`, with a hyphen**, while its
  storage file is `.storage/lovelace.dashboard_test` with an underscore. Using
  the file name in a URL gives a page that loads, answers 200, and renders
  nothing at all — no error worth the name. The truth is in
  `.storage/lovelace_dashboards`.
- **The panel view wraps the card in `hui-card-options`; the sections view wraps
  it in `hui-card-edit-mode`.** Both are excluded by `_inEditPreview()`, so this
  explains nothing on its own — but it proves panel and sections are different
  code paths in Home Assistant, not merely different widths. Entry 11 of
  `mem:picture-studio/follow-ups` already wanted the two views to carry the same
  card config precisely so this axis could be measured.

## Where it is stuck: getting the phone to the local HA

The measurement needs the phone on the local Home Assistant, which serves the
instrumented build. The chain, and it has one broken link:

- HA publishes `8123` on `0.0.0.0` **of the devcontainer**, whose only address
  is `192.168.215.4` — an OrbStack private network, invisible from the LAN.
  Verified from inside: `curl localhost:8123` → 200, `ss -ltn` → `0.0.0.0:8123`.
- `forwardPorts: [8123]` in `.devcontainer/devcontainer.json` is what makes it
  work on the Mac, and **VS Code binds a forwarded port to the loopback only**.
  Confirmed by the user with `lsof -nP -iTCP:8123 -sTCP:LISTEN`: `127.0.0.1`.
- The fix attempted is the VS Code setting **`remote.localPortHost` →
  `allInterfaces`**, which must sit in the **User** scope (the Mac's VS Code does
  the forwarding, not the container's), and **does not apply to already-forwarded
  ports** — the port has to be dropped from the PORTS panel, or the window
  reloaded. That reload is what the session was interrupted for.

**The user then asked for the deterministic route, and it is done.**
`.devcontainer/devcontainer.json` now carries `"appPort": ["8123:8123"]` and
`forwardPorts` is **gone** — the two would want the same port on the host and the
loser silently lands on another one. Nothing is lost, since a published port
answers on the loopback as well. The reasoning is written at the edit, because
the next person to see a devcontainer without `forwardPorts` will want to put it
back, and doing so rebuilds the wall this was removed to knock down.

**This change is uncommitted like the rest, and it needs a container rebuild to
take effect.** Two traps on the far side of that rebuild:

- The local Home Assistant runs in a **nested** Docker inside the devcontainer.
  After a rebuild, check `docker ps` for `picture-studio-ha` and bring it back
  with `docker compose up -d` if it is gone. `.ha/config` is a bind mount from
  the workspace, so no configuration is lost either way — but the container is
  not guaranteed to survive.
- `dist/` holds the **instrumented** build and is mounted read-only into that
  container. If it is rebuilt from a clean tree, the instrumentation disappears
  from the phone's view without any sign, and the overlay simply never appears.

## What to do next, in order

1. Get the phone onto the local HA and have the user run **one failing drag in
   the panel view**, then read the black strip. One line names the writer.
2. Only then choose a remedy. The three writers need three unrelated fixes, and
   this project's oldest lesson is that a finding can be right about the
   mechanism and wrong about the remedy.
3. Remove every `// TEMP` touch point and `src/card/drag-diagnostics.ts`, and
   rebuild, before anything is committed.
4. The fix is **1.5.2**. Open it the way `AGENTS.md` rules 4-7 say — and only
   when the user asks for the number in so many words.

## The question nobody has answered yet

**Is this a 1.5.1 regression or older?** It matters for what users are told. A
`restore:cancel` verdict would make it long-standing, since restoring on a
non-commit shipped in 1.5.0. A `rebuild` verdict would also be long-standing.
Only `apply` would put it squarely on 1.5.1's change. Do not write a CHANGELOG
entry that claims either until the trace says which.
